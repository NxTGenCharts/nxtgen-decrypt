How this works — every leg multiplies your balance by the best bid (selling) or 1/ask (buying) available on that exchange's public order book, minus your taker fee. A positive % means the round trip A→B→C→A returns more than you started with, before slippage. All three legs of one cycle always execute on the same exchange — arbitrage never mixes order books mid-cycle. Fiat on/off-ramp pairs (USDT/TRY, USDC/IDR, etc.) and any leg under your min 24h volume are excluded before the graph is even built, the same two filters the CLI scanner applies — this is what keeps the results to cycles you could plausibly fill instead of noise from thin or non-tradeable pairs.
Theoretical (CLI-style) toggle — checking this reproduces the bundled CLI scanner's math exactly: one last-traded price for both the buy and sell leg (no bid/ask spread) and no fee subtracted. It exists so you can compare the two side by side — it is not a more accurate mode, it's the CLI's shortcut that makes its numbers look better than a real fill would be. Leave it off for a realistic read.
Live Scan — re-scans on the interval you set and tags each row NEW the first time it's seen and OPEN on every pass after that, with a first-seen timestamp — mirroring the CLI scanner's continuous-tracking output. Cycles that stop clearing the filter are logged to a closed strip above the results with how long they stayed open, then drop off.
Read before trading — this is an informational scan of top-of-book prices, not a guarantee, and it does not manufacture profit that isn't really there. Real fills depend on order book depth, execution speed, and simultaneous availability of all three legs — thin books and latency routinely erase small or negative edges either way. This is not financial advice.

How this works — for every asset traded on 2+ of the selected exchanges, this finds the lowest ask (buy side) and the highest bid (sell side) and computes the spread after two taker fees. It requires the buy and sell exchange to be different — a gap on one exchange against itself isn't arbitrage.
Window, Liquidity — Window is how long this session's scanner has continuously seen the gap ("just opened" under ~90s). Liquidity compares top-of-book depth and 24h volume on both sides against your trade amount.
D/W icons — the small D and W badges next to each exchange show whether this coin is Depositable / Withdrawable on that specific exchange. This is anchored to Bitget's public coin/network status: a green check or red cross next to a Bitget leg is a real, verified answer. Binance and Bybit don't expose network status without an authenticated account, so their D/W badges show a grey "?" ("unverified") rather than a guess — use the ↗ link next to each exchange to check directly on their site before moving funds.
Advanced Filters — narrows what's currently displayed (liquidity, window, quote asset, exchanges included, and a "D/W confirmed only" toggle for routes Bitget's data confirms are transferable) without re-scanning the exchanges.
Read before trading — a live spread here is not automatically capturable: this scans price gaps only, and assumes quote-asset balance is already sitting on the buy exchange and base asset already sitting on the sell exchange — an actual on-chain or internal transfer takes minutes, and the gap is usually gone by the time it lands. Transfer time, withdrawal fees/limits, and the spread closing before you move funds are the main reasons a real gap this size rarely survives long enough to execute risk-free. Cost Breakdown's slippage and gas figures are estimates, not quotes. This is not financial advice.

Scope — Autotrade only trades Triangular Arbitrage, only on Spot markets, and only on the single exchange selected above. Cross-Exchange scanning is not included. Futures trading now lives in its own AI Futures Engine tab (paper mode).
How it decides — each pass, it ranks every triangular cycle on the selected exchange and, if the single most profitable one clears both your min-profit field and the hard 0.8% floor, it "executes" it — this compounds a running day-balance rather than placing a real order (see the notice above).
Daily target — once today's simulated balance is up 11% (or your configured target) from the starting balance you entered, Autotrade stops itself automatically for the day, however many cycles it took to get there, and the summary above is the full record.
This is not financial advice, and simulated results will not exactly match a real fill — depth, slippage, and timing between the three legs all affect a real execution.
PAPER MODE only, synthetic market data. This engine is Phase 1 of the build: the scoring/regime/setup/risk/no-trade logic is real and runs every cycle, but the price data driving it is currently a seeded random-walk generator across a 35-symbol watchlist (see js/futures/mockMarket.js), not live Binance/Bybit futures feeds — that's Phase 2. Nothing here places, or is capable of placing, a real order; there is no LIVE MODE in this build. Whatever win rate / expectancy you see below is whatever the synthetic price path actually produced against these rules — it is not tuned to look good. The active strategy, AI Scalp, trades a genuine 1:1 stop:target; it backtested at a 65-76% win rate on this synthetic feed by trading WITH the mock market's built-in short-term momentum rather than against it — a real, measured result on THIS feed, not a guarantee, and not necessarily representative of how it would perform once Phase 2 swaps in real market data.

Live / Demo Trading ALL FOUR EXCHANGES
Separate from the paper engine above, which keeps running exactly as it always has regardless of what's set here. This places real orders on whichever exchange is selected above using the key already connected for it in Autotrade & Balances — nothing new to connect. Every position opens with an exchange-side stop-loss and take-profit attached at creation (not something this app watches and reacts to), so the exchange itself enforces the exit even if this tab is closed or your connection drops.
Binance and Gate.io both need two extra signed calls per position (their stop-loss and take-profit can't be attached to the entry order the way Bybit's and MEXC's can) — if the entry fills but either of those fails, this app will tell you plainly that the position is open and unprotected rather than reporting a generic error. Check the exchange directly if you ever see that message.
MEXC is Live only — it has no Demo Trading exposed through its API — and its API only gained the ability to place Futures orders at all in March 2026, about five months before this was built. That's not a reason to distrust it (everything here comes straight from MEXC's own current documentation), but it has the least real-world mileage of the four; start smaller and watch it more closely than you would the other three.

Scope — USDT-margined perpetuals across a 35-symbol watchlist. Default risk 1% of equity per trade (configurable 0.1–2%), max 3 simultaneous positions, max 3% total portfolio risk, max 2% daily loss, pause after 3 consecutive losses — all enforced by the No-Trade Engine before any position opens.
Win rate vs. expectancy — the dashboard reports whatever win rate and profit factor the session's trades actually produced. A high win rate with poor expectancy is not something this build is tuned toward, and it will show a losing session honestly if that's what happens against the current (synthetic, Paper-mode) price data.
Live/Demo trading (Bybit, Binance, Gate.io, and MEXC — MEXC is Live only, no Demo) places real orders through server/server.js with a native/exchange-enforced stop-loss and take-profit on every position — see the Live/Demo Trading section above and server/README.md before arming it with a Live key.
What's next — real historical backtesting, walk-forward optimization, and Monte Carlo analysis all require actual historical OHLCV + a place to store it, which this static app doesn't have yet. All four exchanges now have Live/Demo (or Live-only, for MEXC) execution built.
